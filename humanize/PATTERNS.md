# Humanization patterns

Treat these as warning signs. Fix the weak meaning or repeated habit instead of hiding a tell with a synonym. Preserve deliberate style choices.

## Empty framing and promotion

State what happened or what the subject does. Replace claims of importance, broad trends, praise, and brochure language with facts that support them. Cut stock openings, challenges, outlooks, and conclusions when they add no information.

`The pivotal platform delivers a seamless experience` becomes `The platform imports invoices, flags duplicate charges, and exports CSV.`

## Commentary attached to facts

An `-ing` phrase often claims significance without explaining a consequence. Name the consequence or stop after the fact.

`The station has six platforms, underscoring its importance` becomes `The station has six platforms and handles 42 scheduled trains each day.`

## Attribution and missing information

Name the source and the evidence behind a claim. When information is absent, state what was checked and what it did not establish. Speculation does not become evidence when attributed to unnamed experts, reports, critics, or observers.

`Details are scarce because she values privacy` becomes `The two biographies reviewed do not discuss her early life.`

## Inflated vocabulary and metaphors

Prefer plain verbs and the real technical concept. Words such as `leverage`, `delve`, `pivotal`, `robust`, `landscape`, `tapestry`, `substrate`, `vector`, `north star`, and `flywheel` deserve scrutiny when they replace a concrete action or mechanism.

`This service provides a robust orchestration substrate` becomes `This service runs the three import steps in order.`

## Formulaic shapes

Use contrast, grouping, ranges, and repetition only when the meaning requires them. Check for `not just X, but Y`, forced groups of three, endpoints that do not form a real scale, and synonym cycling that renames one subject.

`The course covers everything from syntax to teamwork` becomes `The course covers syntax, debugging, and code review.`

## Formatting and punctuation habits

Match the writer's or publication's style. Vary sentence construction instead of leaning on repeated em dashes, semicolons, colons, parentheses, bold labels, headings, or identical bullet shapes. Keep punctuation that carries meaning and formatting that helps navigation.

`**Performance:** Performance improved significantly` becomes `Processing time fell from 18 minutes to 6 minutes.`

## Chat scaffolding and mechanical residue

Remove greetings, praise, prompt narration, generic offers, placeholders, knowledge-cutoff disclaimers, duplicated conclusions, escaped control characters, and template instructions unless the finished artifact needs them. Inspect the rendered or final form, not only the source string.

`Certainly! Here is the requested summary. I hope this helps!` becomes the summary itself.

## Voice and rhythm

Specificity gives prose a voice. When the audience permits, allow first person, a defensible point of view, natural contraction, sentence-length variation, and mild informality. Keep neutral or formal prose neutral or formal. Voice comes from the writer and context, not invented feelings or deliberate messiness.

`This result is concerning` becomes `I would not ship this while retries can duplicate charges.` when that viewpoint belongs to the writer.
